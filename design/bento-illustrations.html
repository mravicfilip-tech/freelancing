Six ways to fill the interface tile

The bento card says User-friendly interface and promises bank payouts in over 30 currencies from a connected wallet. The empty panel inside it is a product window, so each concept below is a piece of real interface, drawn at the size it will actually appear.

What the illustration has to prove

Three claims from the copy: the interface is simple, the money lands in a bank account in a local currency, and the only setup is connecting a wallet. A concept scores on how many of those it carries without a caption.

How to read the mockups

Each tile is a replica of the card at roughly its live proportions. Type inside the panel is the size it will be on a 1440px screen, so if a detail is hard to read here, it will be hard to read there.

The send sheet

What it says. The actual form: two amount fields, a recipient with a bank, one button. Nothing to configure, nothing to learn.

Why it fits. It is the most literal proof of "user-friendly interface", because it is the interface. If the real product looks like this, the illustration is also a promise you can keep.

One motion. The receive amount counts up from 0 to 428.10 once on scroll-in, then holds. No looping.

Watch out. The button is ink, not indigo, so the tile still spends indigo zero times here. Fine, as long as a neighbouring tile spends it once.

Most convincing. Needs the real form to match.

The currency field

What it says. A tilted field of currency codes running past the panel edges, a few filled in ink for the main corridors, one in indigo, and a single pill sitting on top with the count.

Why it fits. Texture instead of a list. It makes the breadth of coverage felt rather than counted, and reads at thumbnail size when the other five would blur.

One motion. The field drifts diagonally at a few pixels per second, like a slow conveyor. Reduced motion stops it.

Watch out. This is decoration more than interface. Use it if a neighbouring bento tile already shows the product, otherwise the headline is unsupported.

Best texture. Loosest link to "interface".

Settled transfers

What it says. A short ledger of payouts: who got paid, at which bank, and how long it took. The top row is still settling, with an indigo progress bar.

Why it fits. Repetition is the argument. Three different currencies and banks, all the same shape, all under a minute. It borrows the "live list of settled transfers" idea from hero direction 3, so the site stays one system.

One motion. The progress bar fills, the row flips to settled, a new row slides in at the top. One cycle every few seconds.

Proves banks and currencies together. Wallet implied only.

Recommendation

Build the send sheet first. It is the only concept that supports the headline literally, and it doubles as a spec for what the product's main screen should look like. Keep it to two fields, one recipient and one button.

Keep the wallet-to-bank line as the fallback. If the real form is not ready to be shown, concept 1 tells the same story with three drawn objects and no UI to keep in sync.

Spread the other claims across the bento. The currency field or the picker belongs in the coverage tile, and the connect screen in the onboarding tile. One tile does not need to carry all three promises.

  • The white panel is the product window from hero direction 3. Hairline border, no shadow, nothing outside it except the lavender horizon.
  • Indigo appears once per tile: the travelling dot, the settling bar, or one currency chip. Ink for buttons.
  • Lime only for a live state, such as the connected dot.
  • Coins and wallets get drawn marks, matching the hero badges, not vendor logos.
  • Numbers live inside sentences and interface labels, never as a stat tile.
  • One motion per tile, looping slowly, and none under reduced motion.
