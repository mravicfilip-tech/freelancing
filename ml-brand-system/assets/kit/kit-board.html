02 Patterns

Summit, Ripple, Slabs and Tally. Redrawn from the concepts you picked.

Each one started as a Weave concept and is now a clean vector built from the mark's own geometry: its real outline, its 60° bar angle, its lever angle and its 2 : 3 : 4 rhythm. Every pattern comes in Carbon, Chalk and Signal, at 16:9, 4:5, 1:1 and as a 1920 × 480 banner.

Pattern 1

Summit

The idea: contour lines climb from the edges to a single peak, and the peak is the mark, outlined in Signal. The business is the mountain; the mark is the top. Every panel places the summit where the layout needs it, leaving room for a headline.

Covers, website heroes, proposal and report covers, event backdrops. Chalk for print, Carbon for screens, Signal for social.

Pattern 2

Ripple

The idea: stripes at the mark's 60° angle, bent and bunched where a hidden lever pushes into them. You see force being applied, which is what leverage is.

Backgrounds for big statements, stage screens, merch, packaging, video transitions. Use it behind one short line of type, never a paragraph.

Pattern 3

Slabs

The idea: a solid field cut into slabs along the bar angle and the lever angle, in the mark's 2 : 3 : 4 widths. A few slabs are pushed out of line, showing the ground underneath, like pieces being levered into place.

Social posts, slide dividers, signage, the office wall. Put text on a single large slab so it sits in the system.

Pattern 4 · from the industry research

Tally

The idea: a count drawn in the mark's own strokes. Three 60° bars and the lever make one group of four, and every stroke is one real call. The Signal strokes, the ones closest to a line at the bar angle, are the calls that were missed and then won back. It is proof, not decoration: swap in the client's real numbers and the pattern redraws itself.

Results posts, case studies, report covers, sales decks. Shown with the example figures 1,284 calls answered and 312 won back.

Social templates

Lever post and Tally poster

Lever post: the photo sits in a window cut at the 60° bar angle, with a Signal bar leaning beside it. Photo and bar together make the mark's rhythm, without showing the logo large. Always use a real owner at work, never stock. Templates ship with a grey photo slot (id="photo") at 4:5, 1:1 and 9:16.

Tally poster: the Tally field with the result set in Host Grotesk, at 4:5 and 9:16.

The example photo is the AI concept image, a stand-in until the client's own photography exists.

Do

  • Use one pattern per layout.
  • Leave the empty side of each panel for the message.
  • Match the colourway to the medium: Chalk for print, Carbon for screens, Signal for social.
  • Let the pattern fill the whole frame, edge to edge.

Don't

  • Put a pattern behind the logo or its clear space. Summit already contains the mark.
  • Mix two patterns, or set long text over Ripple.
  • Recolour outside Carbon, Chalk and Signal.
  • Shrink a pattern into a small tile or repeat it as wallpaper.
03 Service icons

Twenty-four icons, each one moves.

24px grid, 1.5px rounded stroke, matching the mark's round ends. Twelve for the core services, twelve for the rest of a local business's growth stack. Every icon has a three-second motion loop that acts out the job: the missed call rings back, the bars grow, the lever lifts the beam. The motion always settles back on the static icon, and visitors who ask for reduced motion see the static icon only.

Sizes

Drawn at 24px with a 1.5px stroke. Use them at 16, 24, 32 or 48. At 16px the stroke is 1px, so keep that size for dense lists, and keep motion for 24px and up. Don't thicken, fill or recolour parts of an icon.
