# Growth/Performance Agency Branding, Category Clichés, and SMB Trust Signals

> Method note: WebFetch was blocked by the network egress proxy for most target domains (wpromote.com, underconsideration.com, brightlocal.com, financederivative.com, vercel.app brand guides). Findings below come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Treat exact wording as paraphrase and verify quotes before publishing. Findings marked (vendor) come from agencies or tool sellers with a commercial interest.

## 1. What graphic systems do distinctive growth agencies use, and how do they differ from generic agencies?

### Takeaway
The strongest agency identities tie a single symbol to the business model: Tinuiti's Möbius strip stands for "closed-loop" performance, and Brainlabs uses a Mondrian-style grid as a system. Most performance agencies, though, share one look (sans-serif wordmark, circle or abstract mark, "data-driven" story) and set themselves apart through positioning language ("Return of Intelligence", "discovery-led", "Move Faster, Smarter, Together") rather than visuals. I found little published design-press coverage of growth-agency rebrands, which suggests the category is visually under-served.

### Cited Findings
- **Tinuiti** (formerly Elite SEM): the name joins acuity, ingenuity and continuity. The logo is a Möbius strip that reflects the agency's goal of "closed-loop performance marketing". The rebrand followed 40% organic growth and the acquisitions of OrionCKB, Email Aptitude and CPC Strategy — [Tinuiti blog](https://tinuiti.com/blog/news/rebrand/). The rebrand won a Stevie "Brand Renovation of the Year" entry/award — [Stevie Awards](https://mobile.stevieawards.com/aba/tinuiti-re-branding-brand-renovation-year). This is a clear case of a symbol that tells the business story, since the loop stands for attribution and feedback.
- **Jellyfish**: a rebrand with a new logo, a new visual identity and the tagline "Move Faster, Smarter, Together". It launched alongside an AI media in-housing platform after a 12-month AI development programme. Claimed results: campaigns launched 65% faster, infrastructure costs cut by 22%, performance up 30% on average (these are the company's own claims). The CEO framed the identity as combining "left and right brains" — [LBBOnline](https://lbbonline.com/news/jellyfish-unveils-new-tech-new-look); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/profile/the-digital-voice/article/jellyfish-unveils-new-tech-new-look-launches-ai-powered-platform-alongside-rebranding); [Marketech APAC](https://marketech-apac.com/jellyfish-launches-ai-powered-media-in-housing-platform-alongside-major-rebrand/)
- **Brainlabs**: a public brand guide lists the fonts Kanit and Roboto, specific colour palettes and a "Mondrian grid design system". There is also a separate "Design Lab" site — [Brainlabs Brand Style Guide](https://brainlabs-brand-guide.vercel.app/); [Brainlabs Design Lab](https://bl-design-lab.vercel.app/). (Search summary only. The page could not be fetched to confirm details or whether it is official.)
- **Wpromote**: a public external visual brand guidelines PDF (Jan 2026) specifies a wordmark plus a "circle mark", clear space equal to the logo height, and minimum sizes of 72px for the wordmark and 18px for the circle mark. It also covers typography, colours and graphic elements — [Wpromote Visual Brand Guidelines PDF](https://www.wpromote.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Wpromote-Visual-Brand-Guidelines-External.pdf)
- **Croud (UK)**: won Independent Performance Marketing Agency of the Year, praised for a bold repositioning under "Return of Intelligence". It operates in 107 countries — [Performance Marketing World](https://www.performancemarketingworld.com/article/1938778/independent-performance-marketing-agency-year-winner-croud); [WeDiscover list](https://we-discover.com/blog/the-top-15-performance-marketing-agencies-in-the-uk-updated-2026/)
- **Journey Further (UK)** positions itself as "discovery-led". **Impression** leans on "structured experimentation" and "turning evidence into action" — [Journey Further](https://www.journeyfurther.com/en-gb); [WeDiscover](https://we-discover.com/blog/the-top-15-performance-marketing-agencies-in-the-uk-updated-2026/)
- **NoGood** describes itself as "what happens when growth hackers learn design": a hybrid of brand and performance, "AI-native", with named clients Nike, TikTok, MongoDB and Intuit — [NoGood](https://nogood.io/); [NoGood blog](https://nogood.io/blog/branding-agencies/)
- **Power Digital** says it builds brand identities that "tell a story through the lens of data and insights" — [Power Digital](https://powerdigitalmarketing.com/services/creative-services-brand-design-editing/)
- **Brandon** (TBA Worldwide) launched a new identity in May 2025 to signal a move from a traditional agency to an "outcome-driven" partner with a "relentless performance focus" — [Brandon](https://brandon.agency/news/brandon-launches-bold-new-brand-identity-to-reflect-its-next-chapter-of-growth/); [LBBOnline](https://lbbonline.com/news/brandon-launches-bold-new-brand-identity)
- **Data & Marketing Association (UK trade body)** was rebranded by Jack Renwick Studio (2019, just before the time window). "Direct" became "Data", with the central idea "the driving force of intelligent marketing" — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/jack-renwick-studio-dma-rebrand/); [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_data_marketing_association_by_jack_renwick_studio.php); [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/dma-rebrands-data-and-marketing-association/)
- Brand New keeps a "Patterns" tag that collects identities built on pattern systems. It is useful for finding precedents — [Brand New: Patterns](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/collected/patterns)

### Inferences
- Among the named growth agencies, only Tinuiti clearly publishes a symbol rationale tied to how the business works (the closed loop). Most others express their difference in words (AI, intelligence, discovery, outcomes) while the visual system stays generic (a circle mark, a sans-serif wordmark).
- A growth agency with a symbol that encodes its method (a loop, compounding, a funnel turned into a flywheel), extended into a pattern and data-display system, would stand out in a category where design press rarely covers agency rebrands.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the visual details (colours, type, motifs) of the Jellyfish, Wpromote, Hawke Media, Directive, Refine Labs, Lemonlight or Common Thread Collective identities, because pages were blocked or search returned no design coverage.
- I found no Brand New or It's Nice That write-up of a 2020–2026 growth/performance agency rebrand, and no designer credited for Jellyfish's rebrand.

## 2. What are the documented visual clichés in growth/marketing/SaaS/fintech branding?

### Takeaway
Design press and practitioners repeatedly name: the blue-purple gradient hero, "Corporate Memphis" flat people, glossy 3D blobs, identical geometric sans-serif wordmarks ("blanding"), navy fintech palettes with Inter, glowing blue AI brains and network nodes, and stock B2B tropes (handshake, lightbulb, relay baton, rising arrows and rockets). The critique is that sameness produces "generic trust" and "brand blindness".

### Cited Findings
- **SaaS template sameness**: the typical site has a gradient hero, an illustration of happy people on laptops, a three-column feature grid, a "Trusted by" logo bar and a blue-purple palette. The critique calls this "brand blindness" and blames template culture: rounded corners, soft gradients, friendly illustrations — [Overpass Studio](https://www.overpass.studio/blog/why-saas-websites-look-the-same) (vendor); [Metabrand](https://www.metabrand.digital/guides/startup-branding-guide/b2b-saas-branding) (vendor)
- **Blob and gradient illustration** is described as the 2018–2022 SaaS aesthetic and "dates quickly". 3D "shiny blobs and floating objects" were a tech-startup staple — [Meet Bob](https://www.meetbob.co.uk/illustration-styles-explained-finding-the-right-look-for-your-brand/); [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/design/branding-cartoon-illustration-design-trend/)
- **Corporate Memphis** (flat, long-limbed, blue-skinned figures) has been widely criticised as "soulless", "lazy" and "toxic positivity" — [Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/corporate-memphis); [Creative Bloq, "Why I'm glad the Corporate Memphis art style is dead"](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/corporate-memphis-style-is-dead); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_Memphis). Eye on Design adds nuance: the think pieces turned the style into a cautionary tale that reflects illustrators' pay anxieties, while overlooking flat art's long history — [Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/what-the-think-pieces-about-corporate-memphis-tell-us-about-the-state-of-illustration/); see also [Eye on Design on corporate mascots](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/the-internet-is-turning-on-big-techs-colorful-corporate-mascots/)
- **"Blanding" / sans-serif homogenisation**: Google, Spotify, Airbnb and Pinterest moved to near-identical geometric sans logos. D&AD and others describe a backlash and a move toward serifs and custom type — [Artsy](https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-one-trend-dominates-logos-big-tech); [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/insights/features/beyond-sans-serif-how-type-can-move-blanding-awards-insights); [Design & Paper](https://www.designandpaper.com/they-are-calling-it-a-sans-serif-backlash-designers-grow-tired-of-the-homogenisation-of-fonts-in-branding/); [Typogram](https://typogram.co/blog/tech-startup-logos/)
- **Rebrands converging generally**: It's Nice That (Sept 2023) links look-alike rebrands to shortened turnarounds and economic pressure — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/features/why-do-rebrands-all-look-the-same-thematic-graphic-design-120923); [Creativepool](https://creativepool.com/magazine/features/why-are-rebrands-all-starting-to-look-the-same.29615)
- **Fintech sameness**: navy palettes, geometric sans-serifs, abstract gradients, the word "simple", and dark backgrounds with gradient accents set in Inter. The result is "generic trust": a visitor "files it mentally as another fintech that looks legit, then forgets you in 3 seconds". The cause given is a design that has to reassure investors and regulators — [Finance Derivative](https://www.financederivative.com/why-every-fintech-brand-now-looks-the-same/); [Freshly Brewed](https://freshlybrewed.co/insights-news/why-fintech-looks-the-same/); [The Masterly](https://www.themasterly.com/blog/fintech-design-guide)
- **AI and tech imagery**: Better Images of AI names humanoid robots, glowing brains, outstretched robot hands, blue backgrounds and "luminosity/glow" as overused and misleading — [Better Images of AI](https://betterimagesofai.org/); [BIoAI blog, "What does AI look like?"](https://blog.betterimagesofai.org/what-does-ai-look-like/). Stock libraries carry tens of thousands of "plexus" glowing-node network images (for example, iStock lists 34,700+ plexus illustrations), which shows how saturated the motif is — [iStock plexus](https://www.istockphoto.com/illustrations/plexus); [Getty plexus network](https://www.gettyimages.com/photos/plexus-network)
- **B2B stock tropes**: the handshake, the lightbulb, the relay baton, and staged stock photos with "whitened teeth and crisp corporate shirts" — [B2B Marketing, "21 marketing and branding clichés"](https://www.b2bmarketing.net/21-marketing-and-branding-cliches-to-avoid-at-all-costs/)
- **Arrows and rockets** are flagged as cliché territory for marketing and startup logos unless abstracted — [Design Shifu](https://www.designshifu.com/blog/logo-design-for-marketing-agencies); [SitePoint](https://www.sitepoint.com/5-shapes-symbols-and-concepts-to-avoid-in-your-brand/); [FullStop](https://fullstop360.com/blog/insights/branding/9-cliched-logo-design-trends-you-should-avoid). 99designs alone hosts 510+ arrow logo examples — [99designs](https://99designs.com/inspiration/logos/arrow)

### Inferences
- The avoid list for a growth or marketing brand: upward arrows and rockets, generic rising line charts, blue or blue-purple gradients, plexus node networks, glowing AI brains, Corporate Memphis people, glossy 3D blobs and glass, handshakes and lightbulbs, navy plus Inter "fintech trust" styling, and a default geometric sans wordmark.
- Critics agree the problem is not any single motif but motifs that carry no specific story. A chart or loop can work if it encodes the brand's actual method, as Tinuiti's Möbius does.

### Gaps
- I found no strong source from 2020–2026 that critiques "rising line chart" or "3D glass/glassmorphism" specifically in agency branding. Those remain practitioner consensus without a citation.
- I found no Pentagram, Collins or Koto essay that specifically catalogues category clichés.

## 3. What visual approaches build trust with small and local business owners?

### Takeaway
Evidence favours real, specific imagery of actual people, places and work over stock photos. Reviews, especially on Google, and concrete proof such as case studies and numbers also matter. Local businesses also care about their own visibility metrics, like photos on Google Business Profiles. AI imagery is not penalised when undisclosed, but disclosure labels may cut trust.

### Cited Findings
- NN/g eyetracking: users ignore stock photos of generic people. They treat and scrutinise photos of products and of real people (for example, the company's actual staff) as content — [NN/g, Photos as Web Content](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/); [NN/g, 7 Tips for Memorable Imagery](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/7-tips-memorable-imagery/)
- NN/g (2026): 77 U.S. adults saw 6 versions of a fictional consulting-firm homepage. Undisclosed AI hero images rated no lower than stock on trustworthiness, professionalism and authenticity. They scored slightly higher, but only authenticity was significant, and only by 0.4 on a 1–7 scale. Caveat: AI disclosure rules such as the EU AI Act, plus prior research, suggest visible "AI" labels lower trust — [NN/g, AI-Generated Images Can Perform as Well as Stock Photography](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ai-generated-images/); [NN/g PACED disclosure framework](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/disclose-ai-paced/)
- BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2025:
  - 83% of consumers read reviews on Google.
  - Consumers check at least two review sites.
  - 71% read reviews regularly while browsing for local businesses.
  - Consumers are more lenient on review count, and more will choose a business with 0–49 reviews.
  - The share saying star rating does not influence them doubled from 5% to 9%.
  - Consumers equate AI with "fake" yet preferred an AI-written response without knowing it.
  - [BrightLocal 2025](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2025/); [Local Search Forum discussion](https://localsearchforum.com/threads/brightlocals-2025-consumer-review-study.62172/)
- Google Business Profile photos: businesses with photos get 42% more direction requests and 35% more website clicks. This figure is widely attributed to Google data and cited through secondary aggregators, not a primary Google page — [DAC Group](https://www.dacgroup.com/en-gb/insights/local-search-news/why-photos-on-your-google-business-profile-matter/); [ProfileTree](https://profiletree.com/google-my-business-statistics/)
- Local-business advice to replace stock with real people, spaces, products and interactions — [Strong Minded Agency](https://strongmindedagency.com/local-websites-need-local-photography/) (vendor)
- Agency selection: B2B buyers value third-party interactions such as case studies 1.4x more than supplier interactions (Gartner via secondary). 73% of marketers rate case studies among the most effective content (CMI via secondary). Referrals dominate agency discovery, and roughly 40% of businesses consider switching agencies within six months over poor fit or unmet promises — [Twelverays](https://twelverays.agency/blog/digital-marketing-agency-case-studies) (vendor, secondary); [Valpak](https://www.valpak.com/advertise/blog/how-to-choose-ad-agency)
- NN/g lists current, up-to-date content as an enduring credibility signal — cited through [Webie summary of NN/g research](https://webie.com.vn/ai-generated-images-vs-stock-photos-what-new-ux-research-means-for-your-website) (secondary)

### Inferences
- For an agency selling to SMB and local owners, the most credible visual devices are:
  - real photography of actual client businesses (shopfronts, owners, staff);
  - specific result numbers tied to named clients;
  - reproduced review stars and Google-style review snippets;
  - before-and-after comparisons (ranking, calls, bookings);
  - local cues such as neighbourhoods, maps and recognisable streets.
- Abstract tech imagery is likely to read as "not for me". This follows from NN/g's real-vs-stock finding and BrightLocal's review data.
- SMB owners look at proof that mirrors what their own customers see (Google reviews, map-pack visibility). Showing those familiar interfaces as proof may land better than abstract dashboards.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous primary study of how SMB owners (as B2B buyers) visually judge marketing agencies. The evidence is extrapolated from consumer trust research (NN/g, BrightLocal) and from vendor or secondary stats.
- Several stats (Gartner 1.4x, CMI 73%, 40% switching, Google 42%/35%) are secondary citations. The primary sources were not verified.

## 4. Examples of brands that extend a symbol into a pattern system that tells a business story

### Takeaway
The best-documented examples are Tinuiti's Möbius loop (closed-loop performance) and Wise's "fast flag" (money moving across borders), both of which carry a business idea rather than decoration. Brainlabs' Mondrian grid is a system-level pattern, but I could not verify its rationale.

### Cited Findings
- **Tinuiti**: the Möbius strip logo expresses closed-loop performance marketing — [Tinuiti](https://tinuiti.com/blog/news/rebrand/)
- **Wise** (Ragged Edge, 2023): the "fast flag" symbol (money without borders) was kept and recrafted within the idea "The World's Money". Green signals money and the environment. The bespoke Wise Sans typeface draws letterforms from global scripts. The original TransferWise flag came from venturethree — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/wise-rebrand-ragged-edge/); [Ragged Edge](https://raggededge.com/partnerships/wise); [The Brand Identity interview](https://the-brandidentity.com/interview/how-the-ragged-edge-and-wise-teams-came-together-to-craft-a-pivotal-rebrand-for-the-worlds-money); [venturethree](https://venturethree.com/work/wise-rebrand); [ADC/One Club](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/adcawards/-award/51194/wise-rebrand/)
- **Brainlabs**: a "Mondrian grid design system" is referenced in its brand guide — [Brainlabs Brand Style Guide](https://brainlabs-brand-guide.vercel.app/) (unverified)
- **Precedent library**: Brand New "Patterns" collection — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/collected/patterns)

### Inferences
- The pattern that tells a story works when the repeated unit is itself the business mechanic: a loop, a flag in motion, a grid of tests. Tiling it can then show scale, repetition or compounding. For a growth agency, a mark that tiles to show "compounding" or "many small tested wins" would carry meaning, which a decorative motif would not.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm in the time available how Tinuiti or Wise apply their symbols as repeating patterns in collateral. Descriptions cover the logo concept, not pattern use.
