# Fintech and business-finance brand patterns and graphic devices

Method note: research ran on 2026-09-30. Direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy for underconsideration.com, the-brandidentity.com, creativereview.co.uk, plaid.com, brex.com, raggededge.com, redantler.com and bakkenbaeck.com. The findings below therefore come from search-result excerpts of those pages, not full reads. Each claim links the page it comes from. Some sources are low-authority blog or "design-system analysis" sites (blakecrosley.com, refero, getdesign.md, shadcn.io, fintechbranding.studio, financederivative.com, designcompass.org). Those are flagged as secondary where used.

## What patterns and graphic devices does each brand use?

### Takeaway
Each brand's system is built around one "hero" device. Stripe has the animated mesh gradient and, since 2025, a single-stripe symbol. Wise has the Fast Flag, a citrus green and banknote "tapestries". Plaid has guilloche currency engraving. Brex has the flag mark, curves and orange colour blocking. Ramp uses black-and-white editorial with one highlighter-yellow accent. Mercury has cinematic photography, a proprietary typeface and risograph or oil-paint textures. Monzo Business uses the hot coral card edge as a framing device. Klarna has pink, and Block/Square uses 3D cubes. Brands that borrow from the look of banknotes and money (Plaid, Wise) have moved furthest away from generic tech-gradient styling.

### Cited Findings

**Stripe (gradient mesh; 2025 brand refresh)**
- The homepage gradient animation uses WebGL and Canvas, through a minimal WebGL library ("minigl") and a Gradient class that controls the animation properties. A ScrollObserver turns the effect off when it is outside the viewport. Developers have widely reverse-engineered it — [Kevin Hufnagl](https://kevinhufnagl.com/how-to-stripe-website-gradient-effect/); [Bram.us](https://www.bram.us/2021/10/13/how-to-create-the-stripe-website-gradient-effect/); [GitHub wave-gradient](https://github.com/sa3dany/wave-gradient)
- The effect was turned into public tools, for example whatamesh.vercel.app for generating "Stripe-style" gradients, and a roughly 10kb, roughly 800-line package reused by many companies — [Kevin Hufnagl](https://kevinhufnagl.com/how-to-stripe-website-gradient-effect/); [Medium / Bootcamp](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/moving-mesh-gradient-background-with-stripe-mesh-gradient-webgl-package-6dc1c69c4fa2)
- Gradient palette as described by a secondary source: "cream, sherbet orange, lavender, indigo, and ruby pink washed across the upper third of nearly every marketing page", plus one indigo accent (#533afd) and the Söhne typeface at weight 300 — [WeLoveDaily](https://www.welovedaily.net/article/stripe-design-empire-beyond-payments); [shadcn.io Stripe analysis](https://www.shadcn.io/design/stripe) (secondary)
- 2025 refresh: the slanted "S" app icon was replaced by a single white diagonal line on a blue square. The line comes from the slanted square that replaced the dot on the "i" in the 2016 wordmark. The stated aim is small-screen and cross-platform legibility — [Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/09/25/extremely-minimal-new-symbol-of-stripe/) (secondary); a Stripe designer's post that "the Stripe brand identity update is now live" — [LinkedIn, Julian Rotondo](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/julianrotondo_the-stripe-brand-identity-update-is-now-live-activity-7383886704929255425-ND-7)
- The studio Landscape designed a family of Stripe product logos as part of the refresh — [Landscape](https://thisislandscape.com/projects/stripe-product-logos/)
- A Stripe job listing says "Following a 2025 brand refresh, Stripe's visual identity is evolving" — [Stripe Jobs](https://stripe.com/jobs/listing/brand-designer-identity/7769564)

**Brex (Gretel 2020 identity; Studio Freight 2025 refresh)**
- Older identity, 2020, by Gretel: the system centres on a flag logomark "alluding to a rippling flag", meant to represent the "entrepreneurial spirit" of customers. It was designed to speak to founders emotionally while still being ready for mainstream recognition — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_brex_by_gretel.php); [Gretel](https://gretelny.com/press/2021/1/19/transform-brex); [Brex Journal](https://www.brex.com/journal/rebranding-brex)
- Current refresh, 2025, by Studio Freight. It followed three years of Studio Freight work for Brex on employer brand, launches and campaigns, and responded to "exponential growth... straining its visual identity" — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/studio-freights-intimate-knowledge-of-brex-drives-its-smart-rebrand); [Studio Freight](https://studiofreight.com/work/brex)
- Colour: the old orange "could feel like Halloween", especially next to black. Studio Freight rebalanced the neutrals and fixed one definitive orange. It also built a **colour-blocking system that works as both palette and guideline**, setting the ratio of orange to the other colours — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/studio-freights-intimate-knowledge-of-brex-drives-its-smart-rebrand)
- Shape language: **curves** taken from the flag logo became a recurring element across layouts, type treatments and photo direction. Typography is Inter — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/studio-freights-intimate-knowledge-of-brex-drives-its-smart-rebrand); [Threads @thebrandidentity](https://www.threads.com/@thebrandidentity/post/DNV4Eh8S36L/studiofreights-refresh-of-brexs-identity-proves-that-the-best-brand-work-often-c)
- Studio Freight also made a separate Brex AI sub-identity that shows the "multi-faceted yet 'invisible' nature of AI" — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/studio-freight-showcase-the-multi-faceted-yet-invisible-nature-of-ai-in-their-identity-for-brex-ai)

**Ramp (Red Antler identity; Bakken & Baeck "Bento box" product-graphic system)**
- Red Antler's strategy line was "Smart is the new platinum": status comes from visibility, control and data rather than a prestige card. The brand pairs "modern boldness and sophisticated restraint". Its "dramatic sense of scale and energizing highlight color speak to exponential growth" — [Red Antler](https://www.redantler.com/work/ramp)
- Bakken & Baeck built a scalable **product graphic system called "Bento box"**, a modular way of showing product UI in marketing — [Bakken & Baeck](https://bakkenbaeck.com/case/ramp) (excerpt only; details not verified)
- Secondary analysis: Ramp works as "a black-and-white editorial system punctuated by a single highlighter-yellow accent" (chartreuse #e4f222) that appears "only where money moves — CTAs, live counters, and active states" — [Refero Styles](https://styles.refero.design/style/b38702a0-75ab-474c-9106-00b624535825) (secondary); via [logotyp.us](https://logotyp.us/logo/ramp/). Note that logotyp.us also describes the logo as "deep teal", which conflicts with the yellow/black reading. Treat that as unreliable.
- XYZ produced a multi-market out-of-home campaign for Ramp — [XYZ](https://wearexyz.com/case-studies/ramp/)

**Mercury**
- Uses cinematic photography with dramatic lighting borrowed from automotive and fashion advertising, and a purple palette chosen to stand apart from the category's blue and green. UI details are finely tuned, for example a font weight of 480 — [Blake Crosley design guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/mercury) (secondary)
- The brand film uses "human-touched textures inspired by risograph printing for characters and oil paint strokes for environments" — search excerpt from [Behance "Banking Should Do More"](https://www.behance.net/gallery/224322531/Mercury-Banking-Should-Do-More)
- Uses a muted palette, generous whitespace and editorial or luxury photography, plus a proprietary typeface (Arcadia) that reads "modern and institutional" — [Striped Horse](https://www.stripedhorse.com/blog/best-financial-website-designs); [toimi.pro](https://toimi.pro/blog/best-fintech-website-designs/) (secondary)

**Wise (Ragged Edge, launched 1 March 2023)**
- Core idea: "The World's Money is for everyone, everywhere". The primary colour moved from "safe blue" to a vibrant citrus green, described as a universal symbol of money and progress — [Tearsheet](https://tearsheet.co/marketing/from-safe-blue-to-citrus-green-how-wise-rebranded-to-an-identity-that-is-exciting-and-good-for-its-bottom-line/); [Wise blog](https://wise.com/en-jp/blog/a-brand-for-everywhere-wise-unveils-bold-new-look)
- The **Fast Flag** icon was strengthened to work as a stand-alone icon, and the wordmark was redrawn for legibility — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_wise_by_ragged_edge.php); [Ragged Edge](https://raggededge.com/partnerships/wise)
- The bespoke typeface Wise Sans takes letterforms from scripts around the world and supports 960 languages — [Creativepool](https://creativepool.com/magazine/inspiration/ragged-edge-and-wise-change-the-worlds-money--behindthebrand.28571)
- **Graphic "tapestries" reference the world's banknotes**, fusing colour, imagery and texture — [Creativepool](https://creativepool.com/magazine/inspiration/ragged-edge-and-wise-change-the-worlds-money--behindthebrand.28571)
- Ragged Edge later rebranded Wise Platform, the B2B infrastructure arm, for a business audience — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/ragged-edge-rebrands-wise-platform-to-better-serve-its-b2b-audience/)
- The rebrand won an ADC award — [The One Club](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/adcawards/-award/51194/wise-rebrand/)

**Revolut (Business included)**
- The 2023–2024 refresh was Revolut's third brand evolution since 2015. It fine-tuned the 2020 wordmark (Aeonik Pro), **expanded the use of 3D graphics** to show product categories, and moved photography to candid, companionship-focused moments instead of POS systems and cards. The refresh is credited to Fintech Branding Studio, which is a self-reported source — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_for_revolut_2023.php); [Fintech Branding Studio](https://fintechbranding.studio/revolut-brand-refresh); [Revolut LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/revolut_explore-our-glow-up-activity-7117841619902488577-3JL6)

**Square / Block**
- Older identity, December 2021: the corporate rebrand to Block stacks square logos into a cube, then twists and abstracts it. The **primary logo is animated**, and the brand uses 3D to create "cinematic and surreal worlds". Square kept its own name and brand — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/29-november-5-december-2021/square-rebrand-block/); [LogoLounge](https://www.logolounge.com/news/square-is-now-block)

**Monzo Business**
- The Monzo Business identity by Studio Output (2020, older) uses the **hot coral edge of the card as a graphic frame** wrapped around photography of real customers. The stated aim was "trust and reassurance" — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/6-12-april-2020/monzo-business-visual-identity/); [Studio Output](https://www.studio-output.com/work/monzo/); [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/studio-out-put-monz/)
- Ragged Edge refreshed the master Monzo brand in 2022 and kept the hot coral card. Guidelines set a hot-coral-first logo system, hot coral, soft white and deep navy as the primary colours, and Oldschool Grotesk with Monzo Sans for type — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/ragged-edge-monzo-graphic-design-021122); [Monzo blog](https://monzo.com/blog/weve-had-a-little-makeover); [Deck.gallery](https://www.deck.gallery/blog/monzo-brand-guidelines-breakdown/)

**Adyen**
- The in-house brand platform "Engineered for ambition" came with new guidelines, a design system and templates, all produced in under six months. The wordmark is a green geometric lowercase face with open cuts in the strokes, which some read as terminal slots — that reading is interpretive and secondary — [Fintech Marketing Hub](https://www.fintechmarketinghub.com/post/adyen-repositions-brand-new-tagline); [DesignYourWay](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/adyen-logo/)
- Godfrey Dadich worked on the US launch, and Formfactor designed the "Greenroom" event identity — [Godfrey Dadich](https://godfreydadich.com/work/adyen); [Formfactor](https://www.formfactor.studio/project/adyen-greenroom-identity)

**Plaid ("The Fabric of Financial Progress", made in-house)**
- **Guilloche patterns** inspired by currency engraving are "the backbone" of the graphic system. The system also has holographic colour treatments, woodcut-style illustrations and new photography. The logo was kept "unchanged" — [Plaid blog](https://plaid.com/blog/plaid-the-fabric-of-financial-progress/); [Fintech Marketing Hub](https://www.fintechmarketinghub.com/post/plaids-new-brand-identity); [Tearsheet](https://tearsheet.co/marketing/weve-been-inventing-new-possibilities-in-finance-for-over-12-years-it-was-time-to-reinvent-ourselves-plaids-heads-of-creative-break-down-the-firms-recent/)
- Rationale: the rebrand reflects Plaid's expansion beyond account linking into fraud, payments and lending decisioning — [FinTech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/articles/plaid-rebrands-to-reflect-expanded-fintech-capabilities)

**Klarna ("Curiously Bold", in-house, 2024)**
- The pink was kept. The full stop was dropped from the wordmark, which was redrawn for digital use. New custom typefaces arrived with new art direction and motion, balanced by off-white and black, with secondary purples, grey and green — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/klarna-brand-refresh/); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/11/01/tickled-pink-how-klarna-s-vibrant-marketing-made-finance-fun). An earlier identity (2022) was credited to DDB Stockholm and in-house — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_klarna_done_in_house.php)

**Shopify**

### Inferences
- Two strategies stand out. Some brands mine the visual language of money itself: guilloche (Plaid), banknote tapestries (Wise), flags (Brex, Wise), card edges (Monzo). Others take on a luxury or editorial look (Mercury, Ramp, Stripe typography). The money-heritage route gives ownable pattern systems. The editorial route relies on restraint plus one accent colour.
- A single ownable accent colour used under strict rules is common across the set: Wise green, Brex orange with colour-blocking ratios, Ramp yellow reserved for "where money moves", Monzo coral, Klarna pink. This colour discipline arguably does more distinguishing work than any pattern.

### Gaps
- I could not read full case-study pages (proxy blocks). Specific details of Ramp's "Bento box" system, Studio Freight's Brex motion system and Plaid's motion are therefore unverified beyond excerpts.
- Shopify: no source found for its current brand system (search returned only merchant-branding content).
- Mercury: no agency or official rebrand case study found. Its description relies on secondary analyses and a Behance project whose authorship was not verified.
- Revolut Business-specific graphics are not documented separately from the Revolut master brand.

## How do they turn data, money movement or growth into graphics?

### Takeaway
Few of these brands publish a formal "data-as-graphic" device. Most show data through product UI shown as brand imagery (Ramp's Bento box, Stripe's UI illustrations), accent colour tied to live figures (Ramp), motion (Stripe's flowing gradient, Block's animated cube, Brex's rippling flag), or metaphors of movement (Wise's Fast Flag).

### Cited Findings
- Ramp's highlight colour and "dramatic sense of scale" are explicitly said to "speak to exponential growth" — [Red Antler](https://www.redantler.com/work/ramp)
- Ramp's accent is reserved for "CTAs, live counters, and active states", so it marks where money moves in the interface — [Refero Styles](https://styles.refero.design/style/b38702a0-75ab-474c-9106-00b624535825) (secondary)
- The "Bento box" product-graphic system packages product UI into modular tiles for marketing — [Bakken & Baeck](https://bakkenbaeck.com/case/ramp)
- Stripe's animated WebGL gradient is the category's best-known "money in motion" abstraction — [Kevin Hufnagl](https://kevinhufnagl.com/how-to-stripe-website-gradient-effect/)
- The Brex flag "ripples" to stand for entrepreneurial momentum, and the 2025 refresh extends its curves into layouts — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_brex_by_gretel.php); [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/studio-freights-intimate-knowledge-of-brex-drives-its-smart-rebrand)
- Wise's Fast Flag and "The World's Money" tapestries express cross-border movement — [Creativepool](https://creativepool.com/magazine/inspiration/ragged-edge-and-wise-change-the-worlds-money--behindthebrand.28571)
- Block's animated cube logo, stacked from its square brands, shows an ecosystem through motion — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/29-november-5-december-2021/square-rebrand-block/)

### Inferences
- For business customers, "performance and growth" is usually shown by real product UI and numbers presented as hero graphics, not abstract charts. "Trust" comes from currency heritage (guilloche, banknotes), restraint (black and white, whitespace) and institutional typography (Mercury Arcadia, Stripe Söhne).
- Arrows and upward charts are rarely an explicit device in these brands' official systems, beyond Wise's forward-leaning flag, which is itself arrow-like.

### Gaps
- None of the sources I could reach documented a formal chart style or data-visualisation guideline for any of these brands.

## Which devices are now category clichés?

### Takeaway
The Stripe-style animated mesh gradient, dark backgrounds with a neon accent, geometric sans-serifs (especially Inter), navy palettes and "money-in-motion" abstract illustration are widely called fintech clichés. Currency-derived patterns and strict single-colour systems are how the stronger 2023–2025 rebrands escape them.

### Cited Findings
- "Fintech brands all look the same because they all chase the same trust signals: navy palettes, geometric sans-serifs, abstract gradients" — [Finance Derivative](https://www.financederivative.com/why-every-fintech-brand-now-looks-the-same/)
- "Navy plus black plus neon accent palettes make brands interchangeable with Mercury, Brex, Ramp, and 40 others". "Abstract gradients and money-in-motion illustration" are a "category cliché that nobody remembers" — [goklimb](https://www.goklimb.com/blog/how-to-brand-a-fintech-without-looking-like-a-bank) / [Freshly Brewed](https://freshlybrewed.co/insights-news/why-fintech-looks-the-same/) (agency blogs, opinion)
- Teams "default to dark background, gradient accents, Inter typography without a deeper brand story" (a "Stripe effect") — [The Masterly](https://www.themasterly.com/blog/fintech-design-guide) (opinion)
- Evidence the Stripe gradient was copied: open-source clones, generators (whatamesh) and a reusable package — [GitHub](https://github.com/sa3dany/wave-gradient); [Medium](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/moving-mesh-gradient-background-with-stripe-mesh-gradient-webgl-package-6dc1c69c4fa2)
- Wise explicitly moved away from "safe blue" to escape category convention — [Tearsheet](https://tearsheet.co/marketing/from-safe-blue-to-citrus-green-how-wise-rebranded-to-an-identity-that-is-exciting-and-good-for-its-bottom-line/)
- Mercury's purple is framed as a deliberate break from category blue and green — [Blake Crosley](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/mercury) (secondary)
- Revolut moved away from imagery of cards and POS terminals — [Fintech Branding Studio](https://fintechbranding.studio/revolut-brand-refresh)

### Inferences
- Other likely clichés, going by the pattern of the above: the floating 3D metal card, tilted-phone UI mockups and glassmorphic 3D objects (Revolut and Block use 3D heavily). This is inference; no source counted them.
- Inter, as used in Brex's 2025 system, is itself named in the cliché critique. That suggests Brex's distinctiveness now rests on the orange ratios and curves rather than on type.

### Gaps
- There is no quantitative study of how often these devices appear; the cliché claims are practitioner opinion.

## What rationale do the rebrand case studies give?

### Takeaway
The common rationale is that the business outgrew its original product, whether a card, account linking or a transfer tool. The brand therefore needed a system that scales across a platform and global markets, while keeping one recognisable equity (flag, pink, coral, logo).

### Cited Findings
- Brex: the platform grew from startup cards into a "comprehensive financial ecosystem", and the brand "couldn't keep pace" — [The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/studio-freights-intimate-knowledge-of-brex-drives-its-smart-rebrand)
- Plaid: expanded beyond account linking, "it was time to reinvent ourselves" — [Tearsheet](https://tearsheet.co/marketing/weve-been-inventing-new-possibilities-in-finance-for-over-12-years-it-was-time-to-reinvent-ourselves-plaids-heads-of-creative-break-down-the-firms-recent/); [FinTech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/articles/plaid-rebrands-to-reflect-expanded-fintech-capabilities)
- Wise: a global "World's Money" positioning, with the rebrand framed as exciting and "good for its bottom line" — [Tearsheet](https://tearsheet.co/marketing/from-safe-blue-to-citrus-green-how-wise-rebranded-to-an-identity-that-is-exciting-and-good-for-its-bottom-line/)
- Ramp: "Smart is the new platinum", status through control and data — [Red Antler](https://www.redantler.com/work/ramp)
- Stripe 2025: simplicity and small-screen or cross-platform scalability — [Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2025/09/25/extremely-minimal-new-symbol-of-stripe/)
- Adyen: "Engineered for ambition" as a platform for all future creative work — [Fintech Marketing Hub](https://www.fintechmarketinghub.com/post/adyen-repositions-brand-new-tagline)
- Monzo Business: "trust and reassurance" for businesses, with small-business owners "front and centre" — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/6-12-april-2020/monzo-business-visual-identity/); [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/inspiration/studio-out-put-monz/)
- Block: the name "acknowledges the company's growth" as an ecosystem — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/29-november-5-december-2021/square-rebrand-block/)

### Inferences
- Several major refreshes (Plaid, Klarna, Adyen, Revolut's 2020 wordmark, arguably Stripe) were done in-house. In-house brand teams appear to be the norm at scale in fintech.

### Gaps
- Brand New reviewer opinions on Wise, Brex and Revolut could not be read (site blocked).
