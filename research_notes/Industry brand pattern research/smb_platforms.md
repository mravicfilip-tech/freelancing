# Brand patterns and graphic devices of SMB / local-business platforms

Method note: every direct page fetch was blocked by the network egress proxy in this session (underconsideration.com, brand.servicetitan.com, brand.toasttab.com, mindbodyonline.com, podium.com, medium.com, designweek.co.uk, creativebloq.com, thryv.com PDF). The findings below come from search-result extracts of those primary pages. Claims about how the websites look (hero UI mockups, review cards and so on) could not be checked against live pages and are marked in Inferences / Gaps as unverified.

## Q1. What patterns and graphic devices does each brand use?

### Takeaway
The best-documented systems (ServiceTitan, Toast, Mindbody, Thryv, Yelp, Podium, Scorpion) are built on a few shared foundations: a simplified symbol that carries meaning (a message bubble, a growth arrow, a movement "m", a discovery "burst"), one warm "action" colour set against a trusted dark or blue anchor, rounded sans type, and illustration or iconography that shows the product in the business setting. Only a few brands publish rules for graphic devices beyond logo, colour and type. Toast is the clearest example: it has rules for illustration, iconography and highlighting.

### Cited Findings

**Podium (messaging / reviews / payments)**
- New logo turns the "P" into a message bubble, because "everything in Podium [is] built on messaging". The wordmark moved to a more circular, sentence-case form that adds "balance and approachability" to the more geometric symbol, for "stability and personal touch". — [Podium: Introducing the new Podium logo](https://www.podium.com/article/the-new-podium-logo)
- Rationale: the previous logo "got lost in a sea of messaging icons that are hard to tell apart at a glance, especially in smaller sizes". — [Podium](https://www.podium.com/article/the-new-podium-logo)
- Podium has a press kit page. — [Podium Press Kit](https://www.podium.com/press)
- Caution: brandpad.io/podium-brand and Sticky's "Podium rebrand" case study show up in searches but appear to be other companies called Podium (for example PA Media's sports-data Podium, rebranded in Nov 2024). Do not attribute them to Podium.com. — [PA Media Group](https://pamediagroup.com/pa-media-group-announces-the-rebrand-of-pa-betting-services-to-podium/); [Sticky](https://sticky.co.uk/work/case-study/podium/)

**ServiceTitan (trades / field service)**
- Public brand site "ServiceTitan Brand Studio" with sections for Core Product colours, typography and links. — [brand.servicetitan.com](https://brand.servicetitan.com/)
- Primary colours are "Titan Blue" and "Skyline", both blues. They "should be among the highest ratio colors in all designs". — [ServiceTitan Colors](https://brand.servicetitan.com/core-product/colors)
- Type: Sofia Pro Bold for headlines, Nunito Sans for body. Font sizes are always divisible by 8 (an 8-pt system), and headline-to-body height ratio is about 2:1. — [ServiceTitan Typography](https://brand.servicetitan.com/core-product/typography)
- Brand resources include product illustrations shared through Figma, icons and palettes. An in-house "Brand Studio" makes visuals, copy, video and web. — [ServiceTitan Brand Studio](https://brand.servicetitan.com/)
- Separate product design system: "Anvil". — [Anvil Typography](https://v1.anvil.servicetitan.com/foundations/typography/)
- A search summary lists ServiceTitan as a client of the branding agency Flagship. This is unverified and no case study was found. — [search summary via wavespace list](https://www.wavespace.agency/blog/top-branding-agencies-services)

**Toast (restaurants / retail POS)**
- Public brand site covering foundations, colour, logo, illustration, iconography and marketing templates. — [brand.toasttab.com](https://brand.toasttab.com/)
- Primary colours are "Lake Blue" and "Toast Orange", used together as a duo-tone in display icons. On full-colour backgrounds, accent colours "create dimension, with key objects highlighted in white". — [Toast Color](https://brand.toasttab.com/brand-elements/color)
- Illustration rules: larger illustrations are "set in context within the restaurant environment". The area or item of interest is highlighted in Toast Orange and must be "central to the story". The "Toast family" of characters represents owners, employees and diners of all ethnicities, genders and backgrounds, built on a modular base (hair, nose and accessories can be swapped). — [Toast Illustrations](https://brand.toasttab.com/brand-elements/illustrations)
- Iconography: a curated set of line icons, front-facing and 2D, each illustrating a product, feature or part of the business. — [Toast Iconography](https://brand.toasttab.com/brand-elements/iconography)
- Logo: sans serif, minimal, "warm color, smooth edges, and playful icon". Product lockups combine the icon with a product wordmark in the core brand font Effra Regular. A circular container is the primary style for the icon in brand and marketing. — [Toast Logo](https://brand.toasttab.com/brand-elements/logo)
- 2026 brand platform "Built for Busy" by Arnold (launched April 2026). The hero spot "Busy" shows a chef calmly dicing onion in a packed kitchen, and the camera pulls back to a view through the restaurant window. The out-of-home work framed real NYC customers' front windows with custom graphics as "living billboards", with media budget redirected to those businesses. — [Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-built-for-busy-nyc-windows-to-success); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260416902872/en/Get-Busy-Run-Busy-and-Stay-Busy-Toast-Brand-Celebrates-What-It-Takes-To-Succeed-in-Business-Today); [Ad Age](https://adage.com/creativity/work/aa-toast-built-for-busy/); [AdForum](https://www.adforum.com/news/toast-turns-busy-into-a-badge-of-honor-with-new-brand-platform-and-national-campaign-created-by-arnold)

**Mindbody (wellness / fitness studios), in-house refresh, c. 2023**
- Covered by Brand New as "New Logo and Identity for Mindbody done In-house". — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_mindbody_done_in_house.php)
- Moved from the legacy ensō / lotus-in-circle mark to the wordmark as the base identifier. The ensō stays in the icon family. The new mark is a "dynamic, fluid 'm'" symbolising movement ("a heartbeat, a breath, a step forward"). — [Mindbody: Moving with Momentum](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/business/education/blog/moving-momentum-our-new-logo)
- Palette: "an array of greens representing growth and vitality" (including spearmint), deep charcoal for grounding and stability, and "a punchy pink" for "passion and play". Colour pairs were tested for contrast and accessibility. Key customers (Orangetheory, F45) gave feedback. — [Mindbody: Visualizing Vitality](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/business/education/blog/visualizing-vitality-our-brand-refresh); [Brandfetch newsletter](https://brandfetch.substack.com/p/mindbody-movement-in-every-curve)
- Public brand page. — [Mindbody Brand](https://co.mindbodyonline.com/brand)

**Thryv (SMB CRM / marketing; formerly DexYP / Yellow Pages)**
- Renamed from DexYP to Thryv on 15 July 2019, framed as "repositioning DexYP as a new company—a SaaS company". This is the older identity. — [Thryv news](https://www.thryv.com/news/dexyp-announces-corporate-name-change-thryv-inc-companys-focus-small-business-software-reflected-rebranding/)
- The 2024 Brand Book (US and AUS PDFs) says the logo's arrow "represents the growth and prosperity of local business". - Palette: Thryv Orange (RGB 255/80/0) for headlines, buttons and CTAs, Thryv Black (18/18/18), White, and Thryv Midnight (41/53/93). — [Thryv Brand Guide 2024](https://www.thryv.com/pdfs/Thryv_BrandGuide.pdf)
- Conflict: one search extract says the logo should appear only in "Keap Green, white or black". Treat it as unconfirmed. — [Thryv Keap Partner Brand Guidelines](https://www.thryv.com/thryv-keap-partner-brand-guidelines/logos/)

**Yelp / Yelp for Business, in-house refresh, 2021**
- Wordmark redrawn with wider curves, keeping the rounded forms. The "burst" symbol was made more open and rounded and less boxy. Burst and wordmark were designed together using similar radii. — [Yelp Official Blog](https://blog.yelp.com/news/introducing-yelps-new-app-icons-and-refreshed-logo/); [Yelp Design (Medium)](https://medium.com/yelp-design/an-inside-look-at-how-we-refreshed-yelps-logo-app-icons-1b1c7f590ee1)

- The burst is described as an homage to the cartoon "moment of discovery" symbol. Brand colours are red, black and white. Yelp provides "Find us on Yelp", "Yelp for Business" and "Yelp for Restaurants" badges for businesses' signage, vehicles and websites. — [Yelp Brand Center](https://www.yelp.com/brand)
- Yelp has a public design system, "Cookbook". — [Yelp Styleguide](https://www.yelp.com/styleguide)

**Scorpion (marketing for home services, legal, franchise, healthcare), 2020 rebrand (older)**

**Square (seller-facing)**

### Inferences
- Symbols built from product meaning are a recurring device: Podium's P as a message bubble, Thryv's growth arrow, Yelp's burst of discovery, Mindbody's movement "m", GoDaddy's heart-shaped GO, and Scorpion's negative-space star for the client.
- A common colour pattern is a trusted cool anchor (ServiceTitan Titan Blue, Toast Lake Blue, Thryv Midnight, Mindbody charcoal) paired with a warm "action" colour (Toast Orange, Thryv Orange for CTAs, Mindbody pink, Yelp red). The warm colour marks the thing to look at: the CTA or the highlighted object.
- Toast's rule of "highlight the key object in orange inside a real restaurant scene" is the most explicit published version of a device that other brands seem to use: show the product in the owner's setting and colour-flag the benefit.
- Rounded, approachable type and form (Podium's circular sentence-case wordmark, Yelp's wider curves, Toast's smooth edges, Thryv lowercase, Sofia Pro and Nunito at ServiceTitan) is category-standard, not distinctive.

### Gaps
- Could not visually verify the websites of Podium, Jobber, Housecall Pro, HighLevel, Birdeye, Hibu or Square (fetches blocked). No sourced description of their hero graphics, pattern systems or photography treatment.
- No agency case studies found for Podium, ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, Birdeye, HighLevel or Hibu. Mindbody, Yelp and GoDaddy (with Lippincott/Koto) were in-house.
- Podium logo article date not captured (likely 2022–2023).

## Q2. Which devices are distinctive and which are category-standard?

### Takeaway
Category-standard: rounded sans type, blue-plus-warm-accent palettes, product UI mockups, and "trusted by N businesses" claims. Distinctive: Toast's owner-centric OOH (real storefronts as billboards) and its modular diverse-character illustration family; Yelp's dimensional "puffy" icons; Podium's messaging-bubble monogram; Scorpion's negative-space star.

### Cited Findings
- Podium explicitly redesigned to escape "a sea of messaging icons that are hard to tell apart", so it recognised generic chat-bubble marks as the category default. — [Podium](https://www.podium.com/article/the-new-podium-logo)
- Toast's "Built for Busy" turns customers' own windows into media ("living billboards"), with budget redirected to the featured businesses. — [Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-built-for-busy-nyc-windows-to-success)
- Toast's modular "Toast family" illustration system (swappable features, representing owners, staff and diners). — [Toast Illustrations](https://brand.toasttab.com/brand-elements/illustrations)
- Yelp's embossed, shadowed icon family was called "divisive" and "glossy, puffy", which makes it distinctive but polarising. — [PRINT](https://www.printmag.com/branding-identity-design/yelp-cooks-up-a-new-look-with-a-divisive-flavor/); [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/yelp-rebrand)
- Scorpion's negative-space star in the stacked lockup. — [Scorpion](https://www.scorpion.co/about-us/scorpion-news/scorpion-news/2020/new-brand-same-commitment-to-clients/)
- GoDaddy's stated move was to "get out of the way" of the customer's story. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90450828/godaddys-new-logo-gets-out-of-the-way-heres-why-thats-good)

### Inferences
- Growth symbolism (arrows, greens, "vitality") is the most common semantic device and so the least distinctive: Thryv's arrow and Mindbody's green "growth" palette say the same thing.
- Brands that stand out use the owner's real place or real business as the medium (Toast windows, Yelp badges on vehicles and storefronts), rather than more abstract graphics.

### Gaps
- No systematic visual audit of web hero patterns (grids, gradients, textures) was possible. Claims about gradients or textures would be unverified.

## Q3. How do these brands show results (numbers, reviews, calls, revenue) to owners?

### Takeaway
Sourced evidence is thin but consistent: scale and trust counts ("200,000+ businesses", "200+ review sites"), dedicated "results" pages, and customer-spotlight storytelling (Toast). How the result graphics look (dashboard cards, star ratings, charts) could not be verified directly.

### Cited Findings
- Birdeye: "Over 200,000 businesses trust Birdeye" and reviews gathered "from 200+ sites". It has a dedicated "Birdeye Effect on Businesses | Customer Success Results" page. — [Birdeye small business](https://birdeye.com/small-business/); [Birdeye results](https://birdeye.com/resources/results/)
- ServiceTitan runs a large case-study library. — [ServiceTitan case studies](https://www.servicetitan.com/case-studies)
- Toast moved marketing spend to customer spotlights, with the business owner as the hero instead of the product. — [Toast newsroom](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/toast-built-for-busy-nyc-windows-to-success); [BriefGlance](https://briefglance.com/companies/toast-inc/pulses/5705)
- Yelp gives businesses co-branded badges ("Find us on Yelp") to display as trust signals in their own marketing. — [Yelp Brand Center](https://www.yelp.com/brand)

### Inferences
- Credibility signals fall into three layers: platform scale (N businesses), third-party proof (review counts and ratings, badges), and owner-level outcomes (case studies). The owner's own premises (windows, vans) is an emerging place to show these.

### Gaps
- No verified descriptions of specific "results" UI graphics (for example Podium review-request cards, ServiceTitan revenue dashboards, HighLevel pipeline charts). The live sites need checking.

## Q4. Rebrand case studies and rationale

### Takeaway
Most SMB-platform identities in this set were done in-house (Mindbody, Yelp, GoDaddy with agency support) or documented only on company blogs (Podium, Scorpion, Thryv). The only agency-led work found is campaign work: Arnold for Toast in 2026, and Lippincott/Koto co-creating GoDaddy's GO in 2020.

### Cited Findings
- Mindbody, in-house (Brand New) — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_mindbody_done_in_house.php)
- Yelp, in-house (Brand New, 2021) — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_icons_for_yelp_done_in_house.php)
- GoDaddy, in-house with Lippincott and Koto (2020) — [GoDaddy press release](https://aboutus.godaddy.net/newsroom/news-releases/press-release-details/2020/GoDaddy-Unveils-New-Company-Logo-the-GO-Expands-Mission-to-Empower-the-Everyday-Entrepreneur/default.aspx)
- Toast "Built for Busy", Arnold (2026), directed by Tim Godsall — [Shots](https://shots.net/news/view/tim-godsall-raises-a-toast-to-the-busy)
- Scorpion, 2020 (interview-led strategy) — [Scorpion](https://www.scorpion.co/about-us/scorpion-news/scorpion-news/2020/new-brand-same-commitment-to-clients/)
- Square kept its identity during the Block rebrand (2021) — [Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/29-november-5-december-2021/square-rebrand-block/)

### Inferences
- Public brand sites (ServiceTitan Brand Studio, Toast Brand, Yelp Brand Center and Cookbook, Thryv Brand Book, Mindbody Brand) are the richest primary sources for this category, more than agency write-ups.

### Gaps
- No Behance, Pentagram, Collins, It's Nice That or Creative Review write-ups were found for Podium, ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro, HighLevel, Birdeye, Thryv, Hibu or Scorpion.
