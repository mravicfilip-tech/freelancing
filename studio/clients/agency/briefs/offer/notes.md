## Rob's method, in the order we run it

1. Pick a market that passes all five tests. Done, scorecard below.
2. Find 8 to 10 pains in the market's own words, from evidence only. In progress.
3. Map the pains against what the four of us deliver. Fill gaps by adding, outsourcing or ruling out of scope. Cut anything tied to no pain.
4. Write the one page offer. Transformation summary on top, components in delivery order, timeline at the bottom.
5. Write the logic chain. The pain, at most 3 steps, then our solution as the conclusion. Step 2 names a category, never our service.
6. Test the chain. Lesson 6 notes are still missing from the module doc.

## Market scorecard

Provisional. Final once the pain research is in.

| Test | Result | Evidence | Weak spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Painful | Weak for now | Depends on the pain we pick. "Nicer design" is not painful. Lost demos or a product that looks less credible than the raise is | Fixed by the pain research |
| Urgent | Weak for now | Urgent only around a trigger, such as a raise, a launch or a pivot | The offer has to hook on a trigger, not on design in general |
| Money | Pass | Funded by definition. DesignMe's floor is $10k per project and $5,900 to $11,900 a month | None |
| ROI 10x | Pass, provisional | A $30k site or product redesign against a $2M to $15M raise, or a conversion lift on paid traffic | We have no numbers of our own yet |
| Reachable | Pass | Funding rounds are announced publicly, and the buyers are active on LinkedIn and X | None |
| $100k question | Pass, provisional | A Series A company buying brand, site and product redesign together. DesignMe runs 18 month engagements for a $72M funded company | Needs proof we can run a deal that size |

Three pass and two are weak. Both weak ones are fixed by the same choice, which trigger the offer hooks on.
