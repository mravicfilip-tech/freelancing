# Competitor research, method 4

Market is funded tech, seed to Series B, across SaaS, AI, fintech and web3. Research date 2026-10-16.

## How this was gathered, and its limits

- Pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises each page. Quotes below are the ones the tool returned inside quotation marks. Spot check any quote before it goes in client facing copy.
- Clutch pages were cut off after about 100,000 characters, so each agency shows a sample of its reviews and not the full list. Review counts read per agency are in the coverage table.
- Funding stage is almost never stated in a Clutch review. Where it is missing I give company type and headcount. This is itself a finding. Funded stage cannot be filtered from public reviews.
- The Clutch link given for DesignMe redirected to the main profile at https://clutch.co/profile/designme-design-no-code-experts. The profile page https://clutch.co/profile/designme was used for the reviews list.
- The Arounda profile at https://clutch.co/profile/arounda-agency returned 404. The working page is https://clutch.co/profile/arounda.
- A web search for complaints on non Clutch sites (Trustpilot, G2) returned nothing about these agencies. Complaints below come from Clutch only.
- Lazarev shows a $100,000+ minimum on Clutch next to a 19 review list that mostly describes small startups. Treat that minimum as unverified.

## Top buying triggers

Triggers are rarely written down. Most reviews say nothing about why now. What does appear, in order of frequency.

1. A launch or a new product with a date. WellSet, Bohr, Vector0, Spatial, My IELTS Guide.
2. A raise, investor conversations or go-to-market. RedOwl, Datawisp, Astraea, Horse Monitor, Upli.
3. A rebrand or new positioning that the product or site no longer matches. felyx, Kitsu, Murf AI, Lazarev's blockchain client, Be GLAD.
4. A product that has outgrown its first version. Encyro, Phenomenon's Financeit, Eleken's Korean dev firm.
5. Competitors catching up. My IELTS Guide.
6. A very short deadline. Half Past Nine, Vestmark.

## Top failed solutions

1. Developers or an engineer led team designing the product. Datawisp, Vector0, Andsend, the Korean dev firm.
2. A previous agency or vendor. Eleken's CPA firm, Ramotion's Vendefy, Phenomenon's WebSec with 5 designers before.
3. Leaving the product untouched. Prism, My IELTS Guide.
4. A site builder or template platform. Halo's NZ real estate client on Squarespace.
5. An internal team that designed the brand. JusTalk.
6. Competing tools or the status quo. Eleken's CRM for freelancers client.

Freelancers and cheap offshore are not named as failures in the reviews read. That absence is worth noting. Clutch reviewers have already chosen an agency, so they rarely tell the story of the freelancer who failed.

## Gaps where clients were unhappy

1. Price and scope shock. Clay goLance 0.5 of 5, Be GLAD change order, Ramotion fintech cost 2.5, Lazarev cost 3.0 twice, Eleken NFT cost 3.0, HackerRank "a little high". Cost is the lowest scoring category at several agencies.
2. Having to repeat requirements and weak project tracking. Ballogy at DesignMe, WellSet at Lazarev, JusTalk at Ramotion.
3. Passive agencies that wait for direction. DesignMe wanted "more proactive inquiries", Ramotion media client wanted them "bolder", Twinkle at Phenomenon wanted more proactive direction.
4. Weak business and product thinking. Phenomenon rewards app client, Lazarev Peel "understood a little bit more of how our product works", Eleken QbDVision wanted senior oversight.
5. Pace. Murf AI at Ramotion, Umso at Halo, Eleken Tidal Cloud wanted time between iterations.
6. Time zone and language. Phenomenon Financeit, Eleken OneCrew, Halo NCF, Lazarev grammar errors.
7. Nothing after launch. York University and Ayalytical at Arounda, Nebraska Realty at Phenomenon.
8. Trust signals. Halo's coaching client could not tell where the company is registered or who the team is.
9. Handoff and ownership. Lazarev's energy client needed help to move Figma ownership, Ramotion's handoff file errors.
10. Honesty about what the agency does. Phenomenon's WebSec client said the studio first said it did not do UX/UI.
11. Minimum commitment. Eleken's two month minimum felt heavy to one NYC CEO.

Overall most Clutch reviews are 4.5 to 5.0, and the critical content sits in the "what could be improved" field, not in the stars. Only one review in the sample is below 4.0.

## What this means for the offer

- Pains 1, 2 and 4 are the strongest and appear at every agency. They map to a funded team whose product looks and works like a developer built first version.
- The weak spots clients write about are cost surprise, repeating themselves, passive designers and no support after launch. Each can be answered with a plain promise, a fixed scope, a named senior lead, a written requirements log and a defined post launch period.
- Public price clarity is a real gap. Only DesignMe shows monthly numbers.
- Evidence gap. Funding stage is missing from nearly all reviews, so interviews or the clients' own LinkedIn and Crunchbase pages are needed to confirm the seed to Series B fit.
