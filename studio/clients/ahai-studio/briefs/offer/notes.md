## Rob's method, in the order we run it

1. Pick a market that passes all five tests. Done, scorecard below.
2. Find 8 to 10 pains in the market's own words, from evidence only. In progress.
3. Map the pains against what the three of us deliver. Fill gaps by adding, outsourcing or ruling out of scope. Cut anything tied to no pain.
4. Write the one page offer. Transformation summary on top, components in delivery order, timeline at the bottom.
5. Write the logic chain. The pain, at most 3 steps, then our solution as the conclusion. Step 2 names a category, never our service.
6. Test the chain in 5 to 10 real buyer conversations where it lands strongly, then lock it. Testing runs alongside the next sections and only has to finish before any website copy is written. Test kit below.

## Failed solutions and objections

What buyers tried before, and what they will hold against us. These shape what we must not sound like.

- Marketplace freelancers. Scam attempts, recycled portfolios, "homework projects", execute but rarely propose improvements.
- A previous agency. Expensive for little value, one founder paid $2k a day, plus passive designers, repeated requirements, cost and scope shock and nothing after launch.
- Remote agencies based in Europe were described by one founder as "moderately successful but the design feels a little amateur". This is the slot buyers already have for us.
- Templates, site builders and developers designing. Looks generic and gets paid for twice.
- AI is now seen as good enough for cheap execution, so we cannot compete on low cost output.
- "For $5000 a month or more, why shouldn't a company just hire the designer directly?"
- Fear that a redesign hurts revenue. One founder's checkout redesign left them 25% off goal.

## Test kit for the chain

Lesson 6. Never pitch and never show the document. Make each point in conversation and watch the reaction.
Calls, client chats and DM exchanges all count, but only the ones where the chain lands. Rob's warning is
about 3 months lost building on a chain nobody tested.

| Signal | What we hear | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | "That's exactly my situation", "I've noticed that too", nodding at each step | On track. The goal is the prospect feeling we understand their business better than they do |
| Lukewarm | "What do you mean by that?", "I'm not sure I follow", a polite "I guess so" | The chain is unclear or wrong |
| Pushback | Agrees with the pain and step 1, resists step 2 or 3, or says it is really something else | That step is where the logic breaks |

Pushback does not kill the chain. Our argument has to beat the alternative they name. The research already
shows the alternatives founders reach for at step 3, so the answers are drafted here. Unproven until a real
conversation tries them.

| Alternative they name | Where it comes from | Draft answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Our developers can restyle it with AI" | "vibecode the style" in founder threads | That is still patching one screen at a time, which is step 1 again |
| "We'll hire a designer" | "why shouldn't a company just hire the designer directly" | A hire takes months and still hands files to the developers, so the date slips |
| "We'll use another agency" | Serg's clients, and the past agency complaints in the reviews | The usual agency is slow, makes a generic look and never learns the product. A fixed date and one team that designs and codes answers all three |
| "A design subscription at $5k a month" | Designjoy named as the model to beat | A subscription works a queue of requests. Nobody owns one finished version by the date |

Where to test, best first.

1. Discovery calls. We have none in the market yet.
2. Past clients. Remittix is the only one in the market. It cannot be a case study, but it can be a test.
3. Content. Posts that run pain, then problem, then solution, judged by comments, shares and DMs. The only place we can test at volume today.

Recently funded founders are reachable for real conversations, since funding rounds are announced publicly.

| Stage | What we reflect back | Strong | Weak or pushback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain | You raised, but the product and site still look like the version your developers built to get here. Fair? | They agree and name the date that worries them | "It's fine for now" |
| Step 1 | Have the fixes so far been patches on top of that first version, one screen at a time? | They list patches or a redesign that stalled | "We just haven't got to it yet" |
| Step 2 | Would it change things if the brand, site and product moved together, before the raise or launch, instead of piece by piece? | They see the whole picture as the fix | They want one piece only |
| Step 3 | If you redesigned today, who would build it, and what would they stop working on? | "Our developers, and the roadmap slips" | "We have a front end person for that". Strengthen step 3 against this, like Eli's coach who wanted better sales calls |
| Offer | What would you need to see before handing this to one outside team? | They ask about price, date or process | They follow the chain but do not want it |
