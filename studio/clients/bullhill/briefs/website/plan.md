# BullHill website, section plan

Status. Proposed 14 October 2026, waiting on Filip's answers and his references. Nothing is built.

## The ask

Filip, 14 October 2026. A landing page for a friend's company. They dig the routes and lay the
ducts that fibre optic cable goes through later. A presentation site that shows their photos and
their work, explains who they are and makes it easy to get in touch. Filip asked for 5 to 6
sections and is sending references.

## Who the page is for

Likely. A fibre trench crew is hired by telecom operators and main contractors, not by the public.
The reader is a site manager or a buyer checking whether this is a real crew with real machines,
the capacity to take a route and a phone that gets answered. Every section answers one of those.

## Sections

One page with anchor links. The phone number sits in a pinned header on every scroll.

1. Hero. Full bleed photo or a short silent loop of their own excavator on a live route, the
   on-black logo lockup, one plain line on what they do, and two buttons, Call us as a tel link
   and Send the job, which jumps to the form. A reader decides in about five seconds whether the
   crew is real, so the best photo they own goes here.
2. What we do. Three or four services as photo cards with one line each. Proposed list to confirm,
   trenching for fibre ducts, duct and chamber laying, road and pavement crossings, surface
   restoration. This list is a guess until Filip confirms it.
3. How a route gets built. One drawn trench cross section in black, red and white, showing the
   ground, the sand bed, the ducts, the warning tape and the restored surface, beside four numbered
   steps, survey and permits, dig, lay ducts, backfill and restore. It explains "dig now, cable
   later" in one picture. It is the only drawing on a page that is otherwise photos.
4. Projects. The showcase and the heaviest section. A numbers band first, kilometres dug, routes
   finished, towns, years working, with only numbers they can stand behind. Then project cards
   with place, route length, year, who it was for and one lead photo. A card opens a full screen
   gallery for that project.
5. Crew and machines. Who they are in three or four sentences, the people with names and faces if
   they agree, the machines by type and size, and the area they cover on a simple map. A main
   contractor checks capacity and coverage before handing out a subcontract.
6. Contact. Phone first and large, a chat app link, email, a four field form with name, place,
   route length and message, company registration details and the service area. The footer
   repeats phone and email.

## What it refuses

- Stock photos, above all glowing blue fibre strands. Their own dirt and steel only.
- A testimonial slider with no real quotes behind it.
- A blog or news section nobody will update.
- Separate pages. One page, anchor links.
- An auto rotating hero slider.

## Open questions, each with a default

1. Who hires them and in which country. Default is operators and main contractors, and the site
   language follows from the country. The English company name hints at clients abroad.
2. Which services exactly. Default is the four in section 2.
3. Photos. How many real ones, and is there drone footage or video. The hero is designed around the
   best shot, so the folder is needed before the direction step.
4. Phone layouts. Most visits will likely come from a phone, after a call or from a truck decal.
   Proposal is to add a 390 phone width to the README Widths line, which overrides the desktop
   first default. Needs Filip's yes.
5. For the direction step. The typeface of the wordmark, so the site type matches it.
