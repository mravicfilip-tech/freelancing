Product thinking

What a visitor must be able to say after scrolling

The visitor is a warehouse or logistics manager, a plant manager or a small business owner with one or two trucks. They do not buy online. They need to know which of the four doors is theirs, and then call or ask for a quote.

The test for every segment is one sentence a stranger can repeat without reading a card. If the animation alone does not carry that sentence, the segment fails.

Najam

I can get a forklift tomorrow for my busy weeks, and they take it back when I am done.

Job to be done. Cover a peak without buying.

Polovni

A used Linde comes back almost new, with a real warranty, for less than new.

Job to be done. Own a truck on a tight budget without the risk.

Novi

Whatever my hall looks like, there is a Linde model built for it.

Job to be done. Pick the right tool as the operation grows.

Servis

When my forklift stops, a van comes to me and gets it running again.

Job to be done. Keep the line moving.

One grammar, four times

Every segment runs the same four beats

Repeating the shape makes the story learnable. By the second segment the visitor knows where to look. The scene changes, the rhythm does not.

1. The problem

Something in the warehouse goes wrong or changes. A pile of pallets, a tight budget, a new high rack, a stopped truck. One short question card.

2. Ekotehnika acts

The service happens on screen. A truck arrives, a renewal line runs, a model changes shape, a van drives across Serbia.

3. The proof

Two or three facts appear as chips, only the company's own claims. These are the reasons to believe.

4. The outcome

The warehouse is moving again. One call to action for that service, plus the rail to jump to the next.

UI, three directions times three versions

Nine combinations from one story

The story above is the same in all nine. What changes is the UI layer on top, the cards, rail, chips and type. Versions 2 Linija, 4 Grad and 5 Sistem keep their own scene and motion. Each gets UI direction A, B and C, so the switcher offers 2A to 5C.

To keep nine affordable, the UI is built once as three themes of one shared card set fed by the same chapter content. Each version then mounts it over its own scene. Nine hand built UIs would be three times the work and would drift apart.

A. Soft sheet

Large white cards on a pale ground

From the first and third references. Very rounded white cards, big light weight numbers with small units after a slash, like 24 h or 6 / 12 meseci, hairline square icon buttons, and the red forklift drawn large so it bleeds off the card edge the way the orange truck does. A dark bottom sheet with a notch tab holds the chapter rail.

B. Glow cards

Dark lower half, cards lit from inside

From the program cards in the first reference and the bank card in the second. Cards carry a soft blurred glow in Linde reds on ink, white type on top, dotted matrix progress for steps like the seven renewal stations.

C. Pills and dots

Frosted cards, a black pill switcher

From the second reference. A black active pill for the four services, small warning badges on problem cards, dot rows for progress and term, and a card carousel that peeks past the edge.

Two things change on the way from these phone screens to a 1440 desktop hero. The references use green and orange, and here every glow and badge is a Linde red or grey token. And the phone layout becomes a wide stage, so cards sit beside the scene instead of stacking.
